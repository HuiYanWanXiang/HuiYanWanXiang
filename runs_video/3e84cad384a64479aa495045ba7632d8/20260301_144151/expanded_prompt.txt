**Animation Plan: Forced Vibrations (12 seconds)**

**Concept Breakdown:**
- Show a simple harmonic oscillator (mass on spring) initially at rest.
- Introduce an external periodic driving force.
- Demonstrate the system's response: transient then steady-state.
- Highlight the amplitude's dependence on driving frequency (resonance hinted).

**Strict Layout Constraints:**
- All objects must remain within the central 80% of frame width and height.
- Text labels and equations are anchored to fixed positions (top, sides) and do not drift.
- When objects move (mass, force arrow), their paths are contained to prevent exiting frame.
- Overlap is avoided by using distinct vertical layers: system at center, labels at top/sides, equation at bottom.

**Scene Steps & Timing:**

**Scene 1: Initial System (0-2s)**
- **Objects:** A horizontal spring attached to a wall on left, a block (mass) at its equilibrium position, resting on a fixed baseline.
- **Labels:** "Mass m", "Spring constant k" placed near respective objects. "No damping" at top.
- **Layout:** System centered. Labels are fixed in position relative to their objects.

**Scene 2: Introduce Driving Force (2-4s)**
- **Objects:** An arrow labeled "F(t)" appears above the mass, pointing right. It begins to oscillate in length (sinusoidally).
- **Equation:** Appears at bottom center: `F_d(t) = F_0 \cos(\omega_d t)`. `\omega_d` is highlighted.
- **Labels:** "Driving Frequency ω_d" appears near the arrow.
- **Layout:** Force arrow's oscillation is small enough to stay within frame. Equation is fixed at bottom.

**Scene 3: Transient Response (4-7s)**
- **Objects:** The mass begins to vibrate. Its motion is initially irregular (superposition of natural and driven oscillation).
- **Animation:** The mass's displacement is plotted in real-time as a growing wave above it.
- **Label:** "Transient Response" appears near the plot.
- **Layout:** The plot appears in the top-right quadrant, not overlapping the main system.

**Scene 4: Steady-State Resonance (7-12s)**
- **Objects:** The mass's motion becomes a clean, large-amplitude oscillation in sync with the driving force arrow.
- **Equation:** Updates to `x(t) = A \cos(\omega_d t - \phi)` at bottom. `A` is highlighted and grows.
- **Label:** "Steady State" replaces "Transient". "Amplitude A" points to the mass's peak displacement.
- **Visual Hint:** A faint, vertical "Natural Frequency ω₀" label appears briefly on the side, suggesting `ω_d ≈ ω₀`.
- **Layout:** The large oscillation amplitude is calibrated to use only 40% of frame width to prevent clipping. All text boxes remain fixed.
