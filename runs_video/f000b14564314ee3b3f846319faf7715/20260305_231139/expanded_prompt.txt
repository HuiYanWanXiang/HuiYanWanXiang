**Animation Plan: Pythagorean Theorem**

**Concept Breakdown:**
1. Introduce right triangle with sides a, b, c (hypotenuse)
2. Show squares constructed on each side
3. Demonstrate area equivalence: a² + b² = c²
4. Visual proof by rearrangement of square areas

**Scene 1: Triangle Introduction (3.0s)**
- Start with blank screen
- Animate drawing of right triangle (thick lines)
- Label vertices A, B, C (C at right angle)
- Label sides: a (vertical), b (horizontal), c (hypotenuse)
- Add right angle symbol at C
- **Layout:** Triangle centered, occupying ~40% of frame height

**Scene 2: Construct Squares (3.0s)**
- Extend side a upward to form square with side length a
- Extend side b rightward to form square with side length b
- Construct square on hypotenuse c (rotated 90° relative to triangle)
- Color squares: a² (blue), b² (green), c² (red)
- Add area labels: a², b², c² inside respective squares
- **Layout:** Squares arranged around triangle without overlapping. Keep all elements within central 80% of frame.

**Scene 3: Equation Reveal (2.0s)**
- Fade out square constructions, keep triangle
- Display equation: a² + b² = c² below triangle
- Animate superscript "2"s appearing
- Highlight each term with matching color from Scene 2
- **Layout:** Equation centered below triangle with 0.5 frame unit spacing. Font size large enough for readability.

**Scene 4: Visual Proof (4.0s)**
- Re-show all three squares from Scene 2
- Animate dissection of a² and b² squares into pieces
- Rearrange pieces to exactly fill c² square
- Show final fit with satisfying "click" animation
- **Layout:** Keep rearrangement within same central area. Ensure pieces don't drift outside frame boundaries during animation.

**Timing Notes:**
- Total: 12.0 seconds
- Smooth transitions between scenes (0.5s crossfades)
- Pause briefly after key revelations (Scene 3 equation, Scene 4 completion)
- Consistent animation speed throughout

**Layout Constraints:**
- All objects must stay within central 90% of frame (5% margin on all sides)
- Text labels positioned with fixed offsets from their objects
- Equation positioned at fixed frame coordinates (0, -2.5, 0)
- During rearrangements, ensure pieces have bounded trajectories
- Final composition should have balanced visual weight
