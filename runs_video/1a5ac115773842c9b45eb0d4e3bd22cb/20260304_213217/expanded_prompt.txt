**Animation Plan: Forced Oscillations**

**Concept Breakdown:**
1. Introduce a simple harmonic oscillator (mass-spring system) at rest.
2. Apply an external periodic driving force.
3. Show the transient response (initial irregular motion).
4. Show the steady-state response (oscillation at driving frequency).
5. Highlight phase difference between driving force and displacement.

**Strict Layout Constraints:**
- All objects must remain within the central 80% of frame width and height.
- Text and equations must be anchored to fixed positions (top, bottom, sides) and not drift.
- The mass-spring system will occupy the left 2/3 of the frame; annotations will occupy the right 1/3.
- Ensure clear spacing between objects to prevent visual clutter.

**Scene Steps & Timing (Total: ~12 seconds):**

**Scene 1: System at Rest (0-2s)**
- **Objects:** A horizontal spring attached to a wall on the left, a block (mass) attached to the right end of the spring, resting at equilibrium position. A fixed vertical reference line marks the equilibrium.
- **Annotations (Right Side):** Title "Forced Oscillations" at top. Equation: `x(t) = 0` (equilibrium).
- **Action:** System is static. No motion.

**Scene 2: Introduce Driving Force (2-4s)**
- **Objects:** An arrow appearing above the block, pointing horizontally to the right, labeled `F_d(t)`. This arrow will pulsate to indicate it's time-varying.
- **Annotations:** Update equation to: `F_d(t) = F_0 cos(ω_d t)`. Appear below title.
- **Action:** The force arrow grows and shrinks sinusoidally in size (magnitude) and reverses direction.

**Scene 3: Transient Response (4-7s)**
- **Objects:** The block begins to oscillate. The motion is initially irregular, not matching the rhythm of the driving force arrow.
- **Annotations:** Add text "Transient Response" below the force equation. The displacement equation updates to a complex form: `x(t) = A e^{-βt} cos(ω t + φ) + ...` (simplified visual).
- **Action:** The block's motion is chaotic at first, amplitude builds up.

**Scene 4: Steady State (7-11s)**
- **Objects:** The block's oscillation becomes regular and smooth. Its frequency now perfectly matches the pulsation frequency of the driving force arrow.
- **Annotations:** Replace "Transient Response" with "Steady State". Update displacement equation to: `x(t) = A cos(ω_d t - δ)`.
- **Action:** Clear, sustained oscillations. Add a phase indicator: a small dot rotating on a reference circle (on the right side, below equations) to visualize the phase lag `δ` between the force (arrow peak) and displacement (block's position).

**Scene 5: Highlight Phase Difference (11-12s)**
- **Objects:** The driving force arrow and the block are both highlighted (e.g., color glow).
- **Annotations:** Text "Phase Lag = δ" appears next to the reference circle.
- **Action:** A brief moment to emphasize the synchronized but offset motion. Freeze on final frame.

**Key Objects List:**
1. Wall (fixed left)
2. Spring (horizontal)
3. Block/Mass
4. Equilibrium reference line (dashed vertical line)
5. Driving Force Arrow (time-varying size/direction)
6. All text labels and equations (right panel)
7. Phase reference circle with rotating dot (for steady state)

**Equations (Displayed Sequentially):**
1. `x = 0`
2. `F_d(t) = F_0 cos(ω_d t)`
3. `x(t) = [Transient Solution] + ...`
4. `x(t) = A cos(ω_d t - δ)`

**Timing Notes:**
- Each scene transition should use smooth animations (FadeIn, Transform, etc.).
- The total animation should be fluid, with a focus on the contrast between transient and steady-state motion.
- The final frame should hold for 1 second.
